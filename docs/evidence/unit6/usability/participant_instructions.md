# Data Speak Usability Session: Participant Instructions

Read aloud by the researcher. Participant ID: ______ (P01 to P05). Date: ____________

## Consent

Thank you for helping evaluate Data Speak, a student capstone application that turns a spreadsheet into a short business report. Participation is voluntary. You may stop at any time, skip any task, and decline any question, without giving a reason. We are testing the software, not you; anything that is hard to do is useful information about the software.

We record only an anonymous ID, whether you work in software or not, what happened during each task, your comments, and your ratings. We do not record your name, email address, employer or any other identifying information, and we do not take screenshots or recordings of you. Your feedback will appear only in anonymous, summarised form in a university assignment.

The data you will upload is a synthetic sales file made for testing; it contains no real customer information. You will use a test account that the researcher has already signed in; you will not need any password.

Do you agree to take part? ☐ Yes ☐ No

## How the session works

There are four short tasks. Please try each one on your own first and say out loud what you are thinking and looking for. If you get stuck, you can ask for help; we will note that help was given. There are no right or wrong ways to use the screen. The whole session takes about 30 minutes.

## Task 1: upload a file

1. Open Data Speak in the browser tab that is already signed in.
2. Create a new analysis and give it any name.
3. Upload the file `retail_sales.csv` that is on the desktop.
4. Tell me: did the file pass the check, and what did the screen tell you about it?

## Task 2: read the data profile

1. Continue to the profile of the file you just uploaded.
2. Tell me how many rows and how many columns the file has.
3. Find any warning about missing values.
4. In your own words, what does that warning mean?

## Task 3: read a finished report

1. Go back to the Analyses list and open the analysis called "Unit 6 usability reference" (it is already finished).
2. Look at the report. Tell me one number at the top that you think a manager would care about, and why.
3. Pick one chart and explain, in your own words, what it shows.

## Task 4: check where a statement comes from

1. In the executive summary at the top of the report, find a sentence with a small link or number next to it.
2. Open "How we know" for that sentence.
3. Tell me what calculation or number supports the sentence.
4. In your own words, what does that evidence mean?
